Consumer Bankruptcy

Chapter 7 Bankruptcy

Chapter 7 is a liquidation bankruptcy that can discharge qualifying unsecured debt — such as credit cards and medical bills — often within a few months of filing.

  • Stops most collection calls, wage garnishments, and lawsuits
  • Can discharge qualifying credit card and medical debt
  • Eligibility is based on a means test tied to income
  • Typically resolves faster than a repayment plan

Chapter 13 Bankruptcy

Chapter 13 sets up a 3–5 year repayment plan, giving you a structured way to catch up on secured debt while keeping the property you care about.

  • Helps you catch up on mortgage or car loan arrears
  • Allows you to keep your home and other property
  • Consolidates debts into one manageable monthly payment
  • An option when Chapter 7 isn't the right fit

Business Bankruptcy

Chapter 11 Bankruptcy

Chapter 11 gives businesses — and individuals with debts beyond Chapter 13 limits — room to reorganize while continuing to operate.

  • Lets a business keep operating while restructuring debt
  • Flexible plans tailored to the business's finances
  • Available to individuals whose debts exceed Chapter 13 limits
  • A path to long-term stability rather than liquidation
